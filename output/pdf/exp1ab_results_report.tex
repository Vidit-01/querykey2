\section{What this document is for}

A research paper has a tight page budget. That budget rewards compact sentences, omitted caveats, and figures that look decisive. Those habits are useful \emph{after} the evidence is already understood. They are dangerous \emph{while} the evidence is still being digested.

This report therefore does four jobs the paper will not have room to do:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Explain the scientific question in ordinary words before introducing notation.
  \item Say what each experiment is \emph{for}, not only what it measured.
  \item Keep every important number next to the interpretation it actually supports.
  \item Separate four kinds of statement: exact mathematics, a measurement, an interpretation, and something not yet established.
\end{enumerate}

When the paper is written, the right workflow is: copy a claim from here, then shorten the prose. Do not invent a cleaner story than the one below.

\section{A one-page map of the four experiments}

The whole program asks a single question:

\begin{quote}
When we initialize a Transformer's query and key matrices, their \emph{relationship} to each other is a free choice. Does that choice create distinct attention behaviors at initialization, and do those behaviors predict early training?
\end{quote}

The four stages answer four different sub-questions:
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2.4cm]
  \item[1A] Is the theory coded correctly? If the formulas are wrong in software, every later map is fiction. This stage \textbf{passed}.
  \item[1B] If we sweep the allowed initializations, what kinds of attention do we actually see? This produced a discovery atlas: a map, not a proof of trainability.
  \item[1C] If we zoom in on uncertain map cells and also try a wider/longer geometry, do ``good-looking'' cells stay good-looking? They mostly do not, jointly. A weaker freeze rule was used to pick eight heads for later work.
  \item[1D] If we freeze those labels \emph{before} looking at training loss, do the ``screened'' cells train better than a pool of ``pathology'' cells on a tiny one-block decoder? Associative recall: no. TinyStories: yes under the preregistered pooling rule, but the yes is mainly a scale effect, not a geometry effect.
\end{description}

\section{How to use this report when writing the paper}

Keep the following compression rules in mind:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Keep the mixed 1D result.} Do not let TinyStories ``PASS'' become ``the atlas predicts trainability.''
  \item \textbf{Keep the $s$-transfer warning.} $s$ is already a multiplier on $1/\sqrt{d}$, not an absolute variance. \end{itemize}

\chapter{Scientific Context and Motivation}
%=============================================================================

\section{The everyday picture}

A Transformer does not read a sentence the way a human does. At each position, it builds a weighted average of other positions. Those weights are called \textbf{attention}. If the weights are almost equal, the model is averaging everyone together (diffuse). If one weight is huge, the model is focusing (concentrated). If the huge weight is usually on the token itself, the model is mostly looking in a mirror (self-locked).

Those weights come from a comparison between two learned views of the same tokens:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item a \textbf{query} view: ``what am I looking for?''
  \item a \textbf{key} view: ``what do I contain?''
\end{itemize}
The comparison is a scaled dot product. If query and key are initialized independently, their typical comparison is one thing. If they are initialized as almost the same matrix, the comparison is another thing. That difference is the subject of this program.

\plainbox{Two models can have equally noisy query weights and equally noisy key weights, and still behave differently, because the \emph{joint} pattern of those two matrices can make attention scores systematically larger on the diagonal, or noisier, or both.}

\section{What problem is being studied?}

Transformer attention computes, for each query position $i$ and key position $j$,
\[
\ell_{ij} = \frac{1}{\sqrt{m}}\,(W_q x_i)^\top (W_k x_j),
\qquad
P_{ij} = \frac{\exp(\ell_{ij})}{\sum_{j'} \exp(\ell_{ij'})},
\]
where $W_q,W_k\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}$ are query and key projections and $x_i,x_j\in\mathbb{R}^d$ are token representations.

The first line is the \textbf{logit} (attention score before softmax). The second line is the \textbf{attention probability}. Softmax turns scores into weights that sum to one.

Standard initializers (for example Xavier or Kaiming on independent Gaussian weights) fix \textbf{marginal} variances: they control how large typical entries of $W_q$ are, and separately how large typical entries of $W_k$ are. They do not explicitly control the \textbf{joint geometry} of $(W_q,W_k)$: whether those two matrices point the same way, point in orthogonal leftover directions, or are unrelated.

The research program introduces a \textbf{correlated Gaussian--orthogonal} family parameterized by $(s,\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ (and an angle $\phi$ that encodes $\beta$ and $\gamma$). It derives \textbf{exact finite-width} expressions for logit moments: formulas that do not require the infinite-width limit. It then \textbf{maps} where different attention behaviors occur in coefficient space, and only afterwards tests trainability on a small decoder.

\section{Why ``geometry'' is not a metaphor for 3-D space}

In this report, ``geometry'' means the statistical shape of the query-key product
\[
M = W_q^\top W_k.
\]
Attention logits are $x_i^\top M x_j / \sqrt{m}$. So the object that actually sets the operating point of softmax is $M$, not $W_q$ or $W_k$ taken separately. Two initializations can match
\[
\mathbb{E}\|W_q\|_F^2 = \mathbb{E}\|W_k\|_F^2
\]
and still produce different laws for $M$. That is the degree of freedom being mapped.

\section{Why staged experiments before training a large language model?}

Training a 32M language model is expensive, noisy, and easy to over-interpret. If we jump there first, we will not know whether a failure came from (i)~a coding error in the initializer, (ii)~an initialization-time attention pathology, (iii)~a scale mismatch, or (iv)~ordinary optimization and data effects.

The stages are therefore blocking questions, in order:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{1A} answers: ``Is the theory implemented correctly?'' If not, every map is meaningless.
  \item \textbf{1B} answers: ``Where in $(s,\alpha,\phi)$ do diffuse, concentrated, self-locked, and screened behaviors occur at initialization?'' This is an empirical atlas, not a hand-drawn phase diagram.
  \item \textbf{1C} answers: ``Do uncertain/boundary cells remain screened under a fitted surrogate, extra samples, and a width/length transfer?'' A frozen portfolio is required before 1D, so that we cannot pick winners after seeing training curves.
  \item \textbf{1D} answers: ``Do screened candidates reduce early training loss relative to pooled pathology groups on a one-block decoder?'' A confidence interval excluding zero in the preregistered direction is required to call the atlas useful for trainability \emph{on that tiny probe}.
\end{enumerate}

Failure of 1A would block all downstream confirmatory interpretation. \textbf{1A passed.} Therefore 1B--1D maps may be interpreted as proposal evidence for the discovery atlas (subject to the limitations in Chapter~\ref{ch:limitations}). The 1C freeze used a documented fallback after the strict intersection was empty. Experiment~1D is complete. Experiment~2, the 32M language-model study, has not been run.

\chapter{Parameterization and Terminology Glossary}
%=============================================================================

This chapter is the dictionary. Later chapters assume it.

\section{\texorpdfstring{Coefficient tuple $(s,\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$}{Coefficient tuple (s, alpha, beta, gamma)}}

The family is
\[
W_q = sA, \qquad W_k = s(\alpha A + \beta B + \gamma C),
\qquad \alpha^2+\beta^2+\gamma^2=1.
\]
$A$ and $C$ are Gaussian matrices with entry standard deviation $1/\sqrt{d}$. $B$ is a random matrix whose row space is orthogonal to the row space of $A$, so $AB^\top=0$ exactly. The four reported numbers are constrained: they are not a free 4-D box.

Each admissible initialization is encoded by:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $s>0$: \textbf{dimensionless fan-in multiplier}. Entries of $A,C$ are drawn with std.\ $1/\sqrt{d}$, then $W_q=sA$. Thus the entry std.\ is $\sigma=s/\sqrt{d}$, and $s=1$ is the architecture baseline (the decoder uses $\mathrm{std}=1/\sqrt{d}$). $s$ is \emph{not} an entry standard deviation and is \emph{not} a size-invariant softmax temperature. Think of $s$ as ``how many times louder than the usual fan-in Gaussian.''
  \item $\alpha\in[-1,1]$: \textbf{entrywise correlation} between flattened query and key coordinates within a head (target marginal correlation of $Q_{ab}$ and $K_{ab}$). Positive $\alpha$ tends to make a token more similar to itself in query-key space, which raises the diagonal logits. Negative $\alpha$ does the opposite.
  \item $\beta,\gamma$: split between a \textbf{row-space orthogonal} component $B$ and an \textbf{independent Gaussian} component $C$ in the construction $W_q W_k^\top = s^2(AB^\top + C)$ with constraints ensuring valid covariance. After the shared $\alpha A$ piece is chosen, the leftover energy must go somewhere. $\beta$ puts it in a direction $A$ cannot see. $\gamma$ puts it in a fresh independent Gaussian.
  \item $\phi\in[0,\pi/2]$: angle parameterizing $(\beta,\gamma)$ on the admissible circle at fixed $\alpha$,
  \[
  \beta=\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\cos\phi, \qquad \gamma=\sqrt{1-\alpha^2}\sin\phi.
  \]
\end{itemize}

At $\alpha=\pm 1$, $\phi$ is redundant (all angles collapse to the same point); the grid retains one endpoint representative per sign.

Four named corners, used later as controls:
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Name & $(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$ & Everyday reading \\
\midrule
Independent & $(0,0,1)$ & Query and key unrelated \\
Orthogonal & $(0,1,0)$ & Leftover energy in $A$'s blind directions \\
Tied & $(1,0,0)$ & Key is a scaled copy of query \\
Anti-tied & $(-1,0,0)$ & Key is a scaled negative copy of query \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\subsection{The two scales that people mix up}

Proposition~1 gives
\[
\mathbb{E}[\ell_{ij}\mid x]=\alpha s^2\sqrt{m}\,c_{ij}
\]
and a variance of the form $\mathrm{Var}(\ell_{ij}\mid x)=s^4(\cdots)$ with a $d$-dependent orthogonal term $g_{ij}$.

There are therefore \emph{two} different ``sizes'' in the construction:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Weight-entry size:} $\sigma=s/\sqrt{d}$. This is what an optimizer sees as the typical magnitude of $W_q$ and $W_k$ entries.
  \item \textbf{Logit operating point:} the typical attention score scales like $s^2\sqrt{m}$ times a token similarity $c_{ij}$. This is what softmax sees.
\end{enumerate}

Copying a numerical $s$ to a new $(d,m)$ therefore changes both $\sigma=s/\sqrt{d}$ and the logit operating point $s^2\sqrt{m}$. Atlas labels attached to $(s,\alpha,\phi)$ at $(d,m,n)=(64,16,64)$ do not transfer to 1D's $(128,32,128)$ or to a 32M LM. A baseline with entry std.\ $\sigma_0$ has equivalent scale $s_0=\sigma_0\sqrt{d}$.

\subsection{\texorpdfstring{$s$ is already a $1/\sqrt{d}$ multiplier, and that is not why it fails to transfer}{s is already a 1/sqrt(d) multiplier, and that is not why it fails to transfer}}
\label{sec:s-not-absolute}

A natural misreading is: ``$s$ does not transfer because it is an absolute variance, rather than a multiple of $1/\sqrt{d}$.'' That is backwards from the constructor.

In code, $A$ is drawn as a standard Gaussian divided by $\sqrt{d}$, then multiplied by $s$:
\[
A_{ab}\sim\mathcal{N}\!\left(0,\frac{1}{d}\right),
\qquad
W_q=sA,
\qquad
\sigma = \frac{s}{\sqrt{d}}.
\]
So $s$ is already the fan-in multiple. It is \emph{not} the entry standard deviation, and it is \emph{not} the entry variance. $s=1$ means ``use the usual $1/\sqrt{d}$ Gaussian''; $s=2$ means ``twice that loud.'' Entry variance is $s^2/d$, which still carries the $1/d$.

The transfer failure is a different fact: one number is being asked to preserve two sizes that scale differently when $(d,m)$ change.

\begin{center}
{\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}p{0.22\linewidth}p{0.35\linewidth}p{0.35\linewidth}@{}}
\toprule
Convention & What stays fixed if you copy the number & What still moves \\
\midrule
Copy $s$ (what 1D did) & fan-in multiple of $1/\sqrt{d}$ & entries quieter as $1/\sqrt{d}$; logits hotter as $\sqrt{m}$ \\
Copy $\sigma=s/\sqrt{d}$ (absolute entry std.) & weight-entry noise & logits still grow as $\sigma^2 d\sqrt{m}$ \\
Copy $s^2\sqrt{m}$ (logit prefactor) & softmax temperature at fixed $\alpha$ & entry noise $\sigma=s/\sqrt{d}$ changes \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}

Making $s$ an absolute variance would not make atlas labels transferable. If entries had a width-independent std.\ $\sigma$ with no $1/\sqrt{d}$, the mean logit would still move as $\alpha\sigma^2 d\sqrt{m}\,c_{ij}$. One scalar cannot keep both the optimizer's weight noise and softmax's operating point fixed unless $(d,m)$ stay put.

The coordinate that would preserve softmax temperature is $s^2\sqrt{m}$ (equivalently $\sigma^2 d\sqrt{m}$), not $s$ and not $\sigma$. That is not the coordinate the atlas stored.

\plainbox{If you remember only one warning from this chapter, remember this: $s$ is already a multiple of $1/\sqrt{d}$, not an absolute variance. $s=2$ at width $64$ is still not the same initializer as $s=2$ at width $128$, because the weights get quieter as $1/\sqrt{d}$ while the typical attention scores get hotter as $\sqrt{m}$.}

\section{Token geometry notation}

For tokens $x_i,x_j$ with Gram entries $v_i=\|x_i\|_2^2/d$, $v_j=\|x_j\|_2^2/d$, $c_{ij}=x_i^\top x_j/d$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $v_i$ is a normalized squared length of token $i$. After LayerNorm-like processing it is often near $1$.
  \item $c_{ij}$ is a normalized inner product, a measure of how similar two tokens are.
  \item \textbf{Predicted logit mean/variance}: closed-form $\mathbb{E}[\ell_{ij}]$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\ell_{ij})$ from Lemma~1 / Proposition~1 (verified in 1A).
  \item $\widehat\tau$: row-centered logit scale (empirical std.\ dev.\ of active logits in a row). Softmax depends only on differences, so the row mean of logits does not matter; $\widehat\tau$ measures how spread out the scores are after removing that mean.
  \item $\widehat\Delta$: \textbf{diagonal gap}---mean diagonal logit minus mean off-diagonal logit on the active set. A large positive gap is the raw material of self-locking.
\end{itemize}

\section{Attention-distribution metrics (1B)}

For each attention row $P_{i\cdot}$ over active keys $k_i>1$:
\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2.4cm]
  \item[Normalized entropy $\Hnorm$]
    $H(P_{i\cdot})/\log k_i$. Equals $1$ for uniform attention; near $0$ for nearly one-hot rows. This is the main ``how spread out is attention?'' number. Dividing by $\log k_i$ makes the metric comparable across causal rows, which have different numbers of allowed keys.
  \item[Distance from uniform $d_{\mathrm{unif}}$]
    RMS $\|P_{i\cdot}-u_i\|_2$ where $u_i$ is uniform on the active support. Small means ``close to equal weights.''
  \item[Maximum mass $\max_j P_{ij}$]
    Largest probability in the row (peakedness). If this is $0.9$, one key owns 90\% of the attention.
  \item[Self-mass $P_{ii}$]
    Diagonal attention probability (self-locking diagnostic).
  \item[Attention effective rank]
    $\mathrm{erank}(P)/n$ where $\mathrm{erank}(P)=\exp(-\sum_k \lambda_k\log\lambda_k)$ on singular values of the probability matrix. This is a matrix-level diversity measure, not the same thing as row entropy. Uniform unmasked attention can have high row entropy and still be rank-one as a matrix.
  \item[Jacobian operator norm $\|J\|_2$]
    Spectral norm of softmax Jacobian $J(P_{i\cdot})=\mathrm{diag}(P_{i\cdot})-P_{i\cdot}P_{i\cdot}^\top$ (local sensitivity / gradient health). If this is tiny, a small change in logits barely changes attention, which can starve learning.
  \item[Update-to-weight ratio]
    $\|\nabla W\|\cdot\eta/\|W\|$ from a single backward step on a synthetic one-batch MSE probe (not full LM training). A diagnostic of ``did anything move?'' after one step.
  \item[Kernel metrics]
    Singular values of $M=W_q^\top W_k$ (operator norm, effective rank, spectral concentration). These describe the query-key product itself, before softmax.
\end{description}

\section{Preregistered descriptive labels}

Labels are assigned from \textbf{95\% bootstrap confidence intervals} (2{,}000 seed-resampling draws per stratum in this run) with precedence
\begin{center}
\small
self-locked $\succ$ concentrated $\succ$ diffuse $\succ$ gradient-starved\\
$\succ$ screened candidate $\succ$ boundary/uncertain.
\end{center}
Precedence means: if a cell satisfies more than one rule, the earlier name wins. Self-locked is diagnosed before concentrated, because a token attending to itself is a special kind of peak, not just ``peaky attention'' in general.

The bootstrap, in ordinary words: each cell was measured with 64 random seeds. We repeatedly redraw those 64 seeds with replacement, recompute the median metric, and look at the 2.5\% and 97.5\% percentiles of that list. If even the pessimistic end of that interval still clears a threshold, we treat the label as confirmed. If the interval sits on both sides of the threshold, we call the cell boundary / uncertain rather than forcing a name.

\begin{description}[style=nextline,leftmargin=2.6cm]
  \item[Diffuse]
    Lower CI bound on median $\Hnorm \ge 0.95$ and upper CI bound on $d_{\mathrm{unif}}\le 0.10$. Translation: we are confident attention is nearly uniform.
  \item[Concentrated]
    Upper CI bound on median $\Hnorm\le 0.50$, \emph{or} lower CI bound on median $\max_j P_{ij}\ge 0.80$. Translation: we are confident attention is peaky.
  \item[Self-locked]
    Lower CI bound on median self-mass $\ge 0.80$. Translation: we are confident the token is mostly attending to itself.
  \item[Gradient-starved]
    Upper CI bound on Jacobian or update-to-weight below pilot floors (see Section~\ref{sec:pilot-thresholds}). Translation: the one-step probe looks quieter than a baseline we measured independently.
  \item[Screened candidate]
    No pathology above confirmed; $\Hnorm$ between $0.60$ and $0.95$; attention effective rank above pilot floor. Translation: not uniform mush, not a spike, not self-locked, and the attention matrix is not too collapsed, according to these screens.
  \item[Boundary / uncertain]
    Intervals cross thresholds (genuine measurement uncertainty).
\end{description}

\textbf{Important:} ``Screened candidate'' means ``passes forward pathology screens,'' not ``will train well.'' Trainability is Experiment~1D; the 1D gate is mixed (Chapter~\ref{ch:1d}).

\chapter{Experiment 1A: Mathematical and Implementation Gate}
%=============================================================================

\plainbox{1A is quality control. It asks whether the code implements the formulas we wrote on paper. It is not trying to discover a new attention phenomenon. If 1A had failed, we would have stopped.}

\section{Purpose}

1A is a \textbf{blocking quality-control stage}. It does not discover scientific novelty; it verifies that the codebase correctly implements:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item exact logit mean and variance formulas (Lemma~1 / Proposition~1);
  \item energy identities and entrywise variance targets (so changing $\alpha$ does not secretly change how large the weights are);
  \item gradient/backprop identities (Proposition~4);
  \item common-bias cross-head statistic (Proposition~5);
  \item model integration (custom initializer, checkpoint survival, forward finiteness).
\end{itemize}

Why each of those matters:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item If the mean/variance formulas are wrong, later talk about ``the theory predicted this operating point'' is empty.
  \item If energies do not match, a comparison between $\alpha=0$ and $\alpha=1$ is confounded by scale.
  \item If gradients are wrong, later backward probes are wrong.
  \item If the initializer does not actually change $W_q$ and $W_k$, or if checkpoints scramble the tensors, later training jobs are not testing the family we think they are testing.
\end{itemize}

\section{Experimental design}

\subsection{Configuration grid}

We vary the things the formulas say should matter, and we include both easy and hard token-pair correlations.
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Widths $d\in\{32,64,128\}$.
  \item Head ratios $m/d\in\{1/8,1/4,1/2\}$.
  \item Token-pair correlations $c_{ij}\in\{-0.5,0,0.5,1.0\}$ (fixed synthetic pairs).
  \item $24$ coefficient points per geometry: four canonical corners plus $20$ interior $(\alpha,\phi)$ points.
\end{itemize}

Total moment configurations: $3\times 3\times 4\times 24 = 864$.

\subsection{Monte Carlo protocol}

``Monte Carlo'' here means: draw many independent copies of $(W_q,W_k)$, compute the attention score for a fixed token pair, and compare the sample mean and sample variance with the closed-form prediction.

For each configuration:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Draw $W_q,W_k$ from the admissible construction at the specified $(s,\alpha,\beta,\gamma)$.
  \item Compute empirical mean and variance of $\ell=(W_q x)^\top(W_k y)/\sqrt{m}$ over increasing draws (minimum $20{,}000$, up to $200{,}000$).
  \item Stop when $\mathrm{SE}(\widehat\mu)\le 0.01\,\sigma_{\mathrm{pred}}$ and $\mathrm{SE}(\widehat{\mathrm{Var}})\le 0.01\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{pred}}$. That is: keep sampling until the Monte Carlo noise is small compared with the predicted scale, so a mismatch cannot be excused as ``we did not sample enough.''
  \item Record energies, orthogonality $\|AB^\top\|_F/(\|A\|_F\|B\|_F)$, entry variances, empirical entry correlation vs.\ $\alpha$.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Hypothesis tests}

For each configuration we form two-sided normal approximate $p$-values for mean and variance. Across all $864$ configurations ($1{,}728$ tests), \textbf{Holm--Bonferroni} correction controls family-wise error at $\alpha=0.01$.

The multiple-testing issue, in ordinary words: if you run 1{,}728 tests at ``5\% significance,'' you expect dozens of false alarms even if every formula is true. Holm--Bonferroni raises the bar so that the chance of \emph{any} false rejection, across the whole family, stays at most $1\%$.

A configuration \textbf{passes} only if:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item neither corrected mean nor variance test is rejected;
  \item precision targets met;
  \item relative orthogonality $<10^{-6}$;
  \item component energies within $1\%$ of $s^2 m$ (and $B,C$ within $1\%$ of $m$);
  \item entry variances within $2\%$ of $s^2/d$;
  \item $|\widehat\rho_{\mathrm{entry}}-\alpha|\le 0.02$.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Auxiliary checks (\texttt{checks.json})}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Gradient identity}: 20 random small configurations; analytic vs.\ finite-difference relative error $<10^{-5}$.
  \item \textbf{Common-bias ratio}: empirical vs.\ predicted cross-kernel correlation ratio; error $<0.02$.
  \item \textbf{Baseline initializer match}: KS test comparing custom independent point to i.i.d.\ Gaussian baseline ($p>0.01$, variance ratio in $[0.9,1.1]$). This checks that our ``independent corner'' really is the ordinary Gaussian people already use.
  \item \textbf{Integration}: initializer changes $W_q,W_k$; checkpoint reload is bitwise-equal; first forward pass finite.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Experiment 1B: Discovery Atlas}

\plainbox{1B is the map-making stage. We no longer look at one token pair in isolation. We run a one-block attention head on whole sequences, measure how attention looks, and name the regions.}

\section{Purpose}

1B empirically maps \textbf{initialization-time attention behavior} across the admissible coefficient domain. Unlike 1A's isolated token pairs, 1B runs a full forward pass through a one-block attention head on \textbf{sequences} of length $n=64$ with value projection and (for the backward probe) a single optimization step.

The scientific product is a labeled atlas: for each allowed $(s,\alpha,\phi)$, under several input and mask conditions, we record whether attention is diffuse, concentrated, self-locked, screened, starved, or still too uncertain to name. That atlas is a measurement. It is not yet a training result.

\section{Logit moments inside the 1B forward pass}
\label{sec:1b-moments}

This section exists because it is easy to misread a diagnostic plot as a second proof of 1A. It is not.

1A tests pairwise moments with many i.i.d.\ draws of $(W_q,W_k)$. 1B stores a \emph{different} diagnostic: for each seed it compares one realized $n\times n$ logit matrix to the closed-form gram formulae. These quantities are not interchangeable.

\paragraph{What is stored.}
Empirical \texttt{logit\_mean} / \texttt{logit\_variance} are the sample mean and sample variance of the realized masked logits $\{\ell_{ij}\}$. Predicted \texttt{predicted\_logit\_mean} is the average of the pairwise conditional means $\mathbb{E}[\ell_{ij}\mid x]$. Predicted \texttt{predicted\_logit\_variance} is the average of the pairwise \emph{conditional} variances $\mathrm{Var}(\ell_{ij}\mid x)$, \textbf{not} the across-pair variance of the map.

The distinction in ordinary words:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item 1A asks: for one fixed pair of tokens, if I redraw the weights many times, do the mean and variance of that one score match the formula?
  \item 1B's stored variance asks: in one forward pass, how spread out are all the scores in the sentence?
\end{itemize}
Those are different spreads. By the law of total variance,
\[
\mathrm{Var}_{ij}(\ell_{ij})
\;=\;
\mathbb{E}_{ij}\!\bigl[\mathrm{Var}(\ell_{ij}\mid x)\bigr]
\;+\;
\mathrm{Var}_{ij}\!\bigl(\mathbb{E}[\ell_{ij}\mid x]\bigr).
\]
The first term is ``each pair is noisy given the tokens.'' The second term is ``different pairs have different average scores, because some tokens are more similar than others.'' The second term is large whenever $\alpha s^2$ is large (token Gram heterogeneity). Plotting empirical across-pair variance minus mean pairwise conditional variance therefore \emph{must} show structured bands at discrete $s$ values. That is an estimand mismatch, not a 1A failure.

\paragraph{The retracted statistic.}
The previous draft quoted mean residuals $-0.0048\pm 0.0484$ and $+0.0003\pm 0.0013$ as ``excellent agreement.'' Those numbers used only the first $20{,}000$ raw rows (cell ids $0$--$39$, the smallest-scale cells). The analyze-script figure itself was drawn on all $879{,}104$ records. The two do not match, and the global mean residual can cancel opposite stratum biases (e.g.\ $r=0$ vs.\ $r=0.5$). This paragraph is left in the long-form report so that the paper does not accidentally revive the retracted claim.

\paragraph{Full-data calibration (this report).}
On all $879{,}104$ single-draw records, OLS of empirical vs.\ predicted is:

Read the ladder slowly. The rule we wanted---screened in all 16 repeated settings---matched nobody. The frozen file is \texttt{candidate\_portfolio.json}. It records
\texttt{frozen = true}, fallback rule \texttt{majority\_reference\_geometry},

This is a \textbf{replication-strictness} fallback, not a new measurement.
Experiment~1D was unblocked by this file plus a separate 1D selection that fills concentrated/self-locked cells from the 1B atlas (Chapter~\ref{ch:1d}).

The paper must not say ``we froze eight heads that replicated as screened across geometries.'' That sentence is false. The true sentence is: ``no such eight heads existed; we froze eight heads that were majority-screened on the reference geometry.''

\chapter{Experiment 1D: One-Block Trainability}
\label{ch:1d}
%=============================================================================

\plainbox{1D is the first time anything is trained. The labels are locked before anyone looks at loss. We ask a narrow question: on a one-block decoder, do the cells we called screened improve faster than a pool of cells we called pathological? The answer depends on the task, and even the TinyStories ``yes'' is easy to over-read.}

\section{Purpose}

1D tests whether initialization-time atlas labels predict \textbf{early trainability} of a one-block decoder. Coefficient points are frozen before any training loss is observed. The preregistered primary outcome is loss decrease per token over a fixed $2{,}000$-step window. The atlas is called useful on a task only if screened candidates beat the pooled pathology groups (diffuse + concentrated + self-locked) with a paired 95\% CI lying entirely above zero.

Why ``loss decrease per token,'' not final loss? Because runs can start at slightly different losses, and because the scientific object is early improvement, not a fully trained model. Why ``per token''? Because the two tasks see different numbers of tokens, and a raw loss drop is not comparable until it is normalized.

Secondary outcomes: divergence, gradient clipping, final train loss, and whether the label predicts failure. This stage does \emph{not} train a 32M language model (Experiment~2). It also does not evaluate held-out perplexity as a publication metric; it is a train-loss probe.

Adaptive 1C cells have no concentrated or self-locked phenotypes (the sampler targeted uncertain cells), so those two strata were filled from the 1B discovery atlas. Labels were assigned before training; \texttt{selected\_points.json} records \texttt{frozen\_before\_training: true}.

\section{\texorpdfstring{$s$ does not transfer across $(d,m)$}{s does not transfer across (d, m)}}
\label{sec:s-transfer}

The constructor is already fan-in scaled: $A_{ab}$ has std.\ $1/\sqrt{d}$, then $W_q=sA$, so
\[
\sigma = \frac{s}{\sqrt{d}}.
\]
This is the same point as Section~\ref{sec:s-not-absolute}. $s$ is \emph{not} an absolute variance. The non-transferability is not ``we forgot to divide by $\sqrt{d}$.'' The $1/\sqrt{d}$ is already in $A$. The remaining problem is that softmax does not see $\sigma$; it sees $s^2\sqrt{m}$.

The 1D decoder baseline uses exactly this $\sigma$ at $s=1$. Proposition~1 then gives a different size dependence for attention logits,
\[
\mathbb{E}[\ell_{ij}\mid x]=\alpha s^2\sqrt{m}\,c_{ij}.
\]
Keeping the atlas number $s$ fixed while changing width therefore does two different things at once: entries get quieter as $1/\sqrt{d}$, while the mean logit prefactor $s^2\sqrt{m}$ tracks head width. If instead one copied the absolute entry std.\ $\sigma$, the weights would match and the logits would still change, because
\[
\mathbb{E}[\ell_{ij}\mid x]=\alpha\,\sigma^2 d\sqrt{m}\,c_{ij}.
\]
1B labels were assigned at $(d,m,n)=(64,16,64)$. 1D trains at $(128,32,128)$ ($d$ and $m$ both doubled; 1C's transfer geometry was $(128,16,128)$, a third pair). Median operating points:

\begin{center}
{\small
\begin{tabular}{lrrrrr}
\toprule
Stratum & Median $s$ & $\sigma$ at 1B $(64,16)$ & $\sigma$ as run in 1D & $s^2\sqrt{m}$ at 1B & $s^2\sqrt{m}$ in 1D \\
\midrule
Screened / corners & 0.777 & 0.097 & 0.069 & 2.41 & 3.41 \\
Diffuse & 0.802 & 0.100 & 0.071 & 2.57 & 3.64 \\
Fan-in baseline & 1.000 & 0.125 & 0.088 & 4.00 & 5.66 \\
Concentrated & 2.000 & 0.250 & 0.177 & 16.0 & 22.6 \\
Self-locked & 2.828 & 0.354 & 0.250 & 32.0 & 45.3 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}}
\end{center}

Transplanting $s=2$ from 1B into 1D \emph{lowers} entry std.\ ($0.250\to 0.177$) and \emph{raises} the logit-mean prefactor ($16\to 22.6$). The 1B ``concentrated'' / ``self-locked'' labels therefore do not name the 1D initialization. Within 1D, every job shares $(128,32)$, so ranking $s$ is still a fair within-model scale comparison; it is not a transferable atlas coordinate for Experiment~2.

A useful translation of the table: in 1D, self-locked fill is initialized with entries about $3.6\times$ noisier than screened fill ($0.250/0.069$), and with a logit prefactor about $13\times$ larger ($45.3/3.41$). That is a different experiment from ``same scale, different query-key correlation.''

\section{Primary gate}

For each seed and task, the contrast is
\[
\Delta_s
=
\overline{\text{loss decrease/token}}_{\text{screened},s}
-
\overline{\text{loss decrease/token}}_{\text{pathology},s}.
\]
Pathology is the pool of diffuse, concentrated, and self-locked points. Boundary and corners are recorded but excluded from the gate.

In ordinary words: pick a seed. Average how much loss dropped per token among the 15 screened points. Subtract the same average among the $15+15+15$ pathology points. Do that eight times, once per seed. The reported $\widehat\Delta$ is the mean of those eight numbers. The CI is a bootstrap of those eight numbers. The gate passes only if even the pessimistic end of the CI is still positive.

\begin{center}
{\small
\begin{tabular}{lccccc}
\toprule

\midrule

TinyStories & $+2.06\times 10^{-8}$ & $[+1.86,\,+2.32]\times 10^{-8}$ & 8 & PASS & $+0.338$ nats \\

\end{tabular}}

Associative recall's $-0.015$ nats is a rounding-error difference over 128 thousand tokens, and the CI includes both signs.

All eight TinyStories seed-level $\Delta_s$ are positive (range $[1.73,2.81]\times 10^{-8}$). Associative-recall seed-level signs split $2$--$6$. Figure~\ref{fig:1d-gate} uses separate $x$-scales because the TinyStories interval is two orders of magnitude tighter than the recall interval.

\section{Associative recall is not a discriminating probe}

Every recall run clips gradients on $100\%$ of steps and finishes at mean train loss $4.172$ regardless of stratum (range of stratum means: $4.171$--$4.172$). Mean total drop is $1.83$--$1.89$ nats. Screened is not better than pathology; the CI includes zero and the point estimate is slightly negative ($-0.015$ nats over $128{,}000$ tokens).

Associative recall, in this implementation, is a synthetic key-value memory task: the model sees pairs and must later retrieve the value given the key. A good probe would separate initializations. This one did not, but the reason matters. Gradient clipping frequency $1.0$ means the optimizer wanted a larger step than the clip allowed, on every step, for every initialization. Final losses sit on top of each other. The task is clip-saturated.

The recall task therefore does \textbf{not} license a claim that atlas labels predict trainability. It also does not license the opposite claim: the probe saturates and is clip-bound, so stratum differences may be invisible rather than absent. For the paper: either retune the probe, or drop it as uninformative. Do not write ``all initializations work equally well on recall.''

\section{What 1D may and may not claim}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{May claim:} under the preregistered pooling rule, TinyStories screened points beat pooled pathology on early loss decrease; associative recall does not.
  \item \textbf{May claim:} one-block TinyStories training is slower to improve from the transplanted high-$s$ 1B fill ($s=2.0$ and $2.83$, i.e.\ $\sigma=0.177$ and $0.250$ at $d=128$) than from the $s\approx 0.8$ 1C band ($\sigma\approx 0.07$).
  \item \textbf{Must not claim:} a numerical $s$ from 1B is the same initializer at another width. $s$ is already a $1/\sqrt{d}$ multiplier, not an absolute variance; the remaining mismatch is that logits scale as $s^2\sqrt{m}$ (or $\sigma^2 d\sqrt{m}$). 1B concentrated/self-locked labels do not transfer to 1D or Experiment~2.
  \item \textbf{Must not claim:} screened geometry is a better initializer than diffuse geometry at matched scale. That contrast is $\approx 0.005$ nats of final TinyStories loss.
  \item \textbf{Must not claim:} the atlas is a validated 32M-LM treatment. Experiment~2 has not been run. The 1C 8-head portfolio is frozen under a fallback, not under the empty all-stratum intersection.
  \item \textbf{Must not claim:} associative recall ``works equally well for all initializations.'' The probe is clip-saturated.
\end{itemize}

No run diverged ($0/1{,}344$). Stability in the ``did not explode'' sense is real, and modest. It is not a quality result.

\chapter{Synthesis and Scientific Reading}
%=============================================================================

This chapter is the one a later paper should compress, not replace.

\section{What 1A established}

The finite-width algebraic theory is not merely analytic---it is implemented correctly in floating point with rigorous Monte Carlo and gradient checks. This is a prerequisite most initialization papers skip. It licenses later sentences of the form ``the code is computing the family we defined.'' It does not license later sentences of the form ``therefore these initializations train well.''

\section{What 1B established}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item The admissible $(s,\alpha,\phi)$ domain admits \textbf{large, structured regions} of distinct initialization phenotypes (diffuse vs.\ concentrated vs.\ screened). The map is not a single blob.
  \item \textbf{Self-locking} is measurable but occupies a minority ($\sim 3.4\%$ of strata).
  \item \textbf{Gradient starvation} at initialization is extremely rare under the pilot floors ($1$ stratum).
  \item Pairwise mean identities carry over to 1B after seed averaging ($R^2=0.996$). Sequence-scale \emph{variance} as stored in 1B is a different estimand and is only approximately calibrated after a total-variance correction (Section~\ref{sec:1b-moments}); it does not re-prove 1A.
  \item Real activations preserve qualitative label proportions but redistribute mass toward concentration relative to synthetic Gaussians.
  \item Many cells sit near label boundaries: a $0.05$ threshold nudge relabels nearly half the atlas in one direction. The continents are real; the borders are thick.
\end{enumerate}

\section{What 1C established}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item A discovery surrogate can be fit and held out with balanced accuracy $0.987$ and ECE $0.015$. The 1B map is interpolable.
  \item The $120$ adaptive cells were fully remeasured at two geometries ($122{,}880$ rows).
  \item Screened labels are \textbf{not} stable under mask, correlation, real activations, and width/length transfer jointly: the preregistered freeze intersection is empty ($0/120$).
  \item Transfer to $(128,16,128)$ shifts mass toward diffuse ($527$ vs.\ $266$ at reference).
  \item A documented fallback froze $8$ heads from $32$ majority-screened reference-geometry points (\texttt{replication\_strict: false}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{What 1D established}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item $84$ points were frozen before training and all $1{,}344$ jobs completed with zero divergence.
  \item Associative recall does not pass the atlas-usefulness gate (CI includes $0$; every step clipped; all strata finish at $4.172$).
  \item TinyStories passes the preregistered pooled-pathology gate ($+0.338$ nats; CI excludes $0$).
  \item The TinyStories pass is a within-1D larger fan-in-multiplier deficit, not a screened-versus-diffuse geometry effect, and not a size-invariant $s$ law. 1B numerical $s$ is not transferable even though $s$ is already a $1/\sqrt{d}$ multiple: $\sigma=s/\sqrt{d}$ and $\mathbb{E}[\ell]\propto s^2\sqrt{m}$. The failure is not ``$s$ was an absolute variance.''
\end{enumerate}

\section{What is \emph{not} yet established}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item An 8-head \texttt{candidate\_portfolio.json} under the original all-stratum intersection (still $0$ points).
  \item Whether screened geometry beats diffuse geometry at matched scale (1D: $\approx 0.005$ nats TinyStories final-loss gap).
  \item Whether a diverse portfolio improves a 32M LM (Experiment~2).
  \item Sign-symmetry unit test on 50 cells (\texttt{sign\_symmetry\_check.json} not present in the full 1B analyze artifact).
\end{itemize}

\section{A candidate paper spine}

If this memoir is later shortened, a defensible narrative spine is:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Joint query-key initialization is a real, energy-matched degree of freedom, with exact finite-width logit moments (1A).
  \item That family produces an empirical initialization atlas with diffuse, concentrated, self-locked, and mid-entropy screened regions (1B).
  \item Screened names do not jointly survive a strict multi-stratum plus transfer freeze (1C).
  \item On a one-block decoder, early TinyStories trainability tracks transplanted scale more than screened-versus-diffuse structure; associative recall is an uninformative saturated probe (1D).
  \item A 32M confirmatory study remains necessary, and must convert scale with $s_0=\sigma_0\sqrt{d}$ rather than pasting 1B's numerical $s$.
\end{enumerate}
Anything stronger than that spine is not supported by this report.

\chapter{Limitations and Provenance}
\label{ch:limitations}
%=============================================================================

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
  \item \textbf{Single-block probe}: no depth, no residual stream, no full LM in 1B. The atlas is an initialization snapshot of one attention block.
  \item \textbf{One backward step}: update ratios are diagnostic, not training trajectories.
  \item \textbf{Bootstrap draws}: 2{,}000 (manifest \texttt{exp1b\_1788602938369\_18144.json}); coarser than preregistered 10{,}000 but sufficient for exploration.
  \item \textbf{CPU/RAM analyze}: $\sim$1.3\,GB JSONL loaded fully into memory.
  \item \textbf{Labels are descriptive}, not causal claims about generalization.
  \item \textbf{1B logit variance is not the 1A estimand}: \texttt{predicted\_logit\_variance} averages pairwise conditional variances; \texttt{logit\_variance} is the across-pair sample variance of one realized map. Pooled mean residuals cancel opposing $r$ and input-kind biases.
  \item \textbf{1C freeze used a fallback}: a point must be screened on all 16 replicated strata to be \texttt{replication\_strict}. Zero of 120 points pass that rule. The frozen 8-head file uses majority screening on the reference geometry $(64,16,64)$.
  \item \textbf{Numerical $s$ is not transferable across sizes, and not because it is an absolute variance}: the constructor already uses $1/\sqrt{d}$ ($\sigma=s/\sqrt{d}$; entry variance $s^2/d$). 1B labels live at $(64,16,64)$; 1D ran $(128,32,128)$. Same $s$ is a different entry std.\ and a different $s^2\sqrt{m}$ logit mean. Copying absolute $\sigma$ instead would still move logits as $\sigma^2 d\sqrt{m}$. Experiment~2 must convert a baseline $\sigma_0$ via $s_0=\sigma_0\sqrt{d}$ and must not paste 1B $s$ as a temperature. See Section~\ref{sec:s-not-absolute}.
  \item \textbf{1D pathology pool mixes scale with phenotype}: concentrated/self-locked 1D cells come from 1B at median $s=2.0$ and $2.83$; screened/diffuse come from 1C at $s\approx 0.8$. Even after $1/\sqrt{d}$, those groups are not scale-matched at 1D.
  \item \textbf{1D associative recall is clip-saturated}: gradient clipping frequency $1.0$ on every recall job; stratum means of final loss differ by $<0.002$ nats.
  \item \textbf{1D is a one-block, 2{,}000-step train loss study}, not held-out 32M LM quality.
  \item \textbf{Git LFS}: raw shards require \texttt{git lfs pull} or manual upload on cloud machines.
\end{itemize}

\chapter{Holm--Bonferroni reminder}

Given ordered $p$-values $p_{(1)}\le\dots\le p_{(m)}$, reject hypothesis $i$ if $p_{(i)}\le \alpha/(m-i+1)$. We applied $m=1728$, $\alpha=0.01$ in 1A.

In ordinary words: sort the 1{,}728 $p$-values from smallest to largest. The smallest one must beat $0.01/1728$ to count as a rejection. The next must beat $0.01/1727$, and so on. This is stricter than uncorrected $0.01$ testing, and it is why ``0 of 864 mean tests rejected'' is a stronger statement than it first appears.

\chapter{Bootstrap procedure in 1B}

For each stratum with seed values $\{x_s\}_{s=1}^{64}$:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Resample 64 seeds with replacement $B=2000$ times.
  \item Compute median metric per resample.
  \item Report 2.5\% and 97.5\% quantiles as CI endpoints.
\end{enumerate}
Seeds are paired across cells; heads/tokens nested within seed are not separate bootstrap units (cluster bootstrap at seed level).

Why cluster at seed level? Because tokens and heads from the same random draw are not independent replicates. Pretending they are would make confidence intervals look narrower than they are.

\chapter{1D paired bootstrap}

For each task, form eight seed-level contrasts $\Delta_s$ as the difference of mean screened and mean pathology loss-decrease-per-token among non-divergent jobs with that paired seed. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
  \item Point estimate: mean of $\{\Delta_s\}_{s=1}^{8}$.
  \item Resample the eight contrasts with replacement $B=10{,}000$ times; 2.5\% / 97.5\% quantiles are the CI.
  \item Gate: pass iff the lower endpoint is strictly positive.
\end{enumerate}
Boundary and canonical-corner jobs are excluded from $\Delta_s$. Implied total-loss gap is $\widehat\Delta$ times mean tokens seen ($128{,}000$ recall; $16{,}384{,}000$ TinyStories).

The eight seed-level numbers are the actual experimental replicates. Points inside a seed are averaged first so that a stratum with 15 points does not pretend to be 15 independent worlds.
